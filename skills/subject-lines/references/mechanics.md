# Mechanics

Check every line against this before presenting it.

---

## Length: front-load the first 30–35 characters

Truncation depends on client, device and orientation. Rough guide:

| Context | Visible characters (approx.) |
| --- | --- |
| iPhone Mail, portrait | 35–40 |
| Gmail app, Android | 35–45 |
| Gmail, desktop web | 60–70 |

You can't control which one a runner is using, and most are on a phone. Write
for the shortest case: **the meaning has to land in the first 30–35 characters.**
Everything after is a bonus, not a payload.

- ❌ "Don't miss out — registration for the 2026 Santa Barbara Half closes Sunday"
- ✅ "Registration closes Sunday — 2026 Santa Barbara Half"

---

## Preheader text is the second half of the subject line

The preheader (preview text) shows beside or under the subject in most inboxes —
roughly 35–90 characters of free real estate, and the most commonly wasted asset
in an email programme.

1. **Never let it default.** Unset, the client pulls the first text in the email,
   usually "View in browser" or an alt tag.
2. **Don't repeat the subject line.** It's a second sentence, not an echo.
3. **Continue the thought.** Subject opens the loop, preheader widens it, email
   closes it.

| Subject | Preheader |
| --- | --- |
| Registration closes Sunday | 1,100 entries left across all three distances. |
| Your finisher photos are ready | Plus your official time, splits and age-group rank. |
| Don't start your taper yet | Three weeks out is earlier than you think. |

Every line you generate ships with a preheader. No exceptions.

---

## The from-name does as much work as the subject line

Runners scan the sender before the subject. A recognisable event name beats a
generic agency or platform name; a human name beats a department.

- ✅ "Santa Barbara Wine Country Half" / "Sarah at SB Half"
- ❌ "noreply" / "Marketing Team"

Keep it consistent across a campaign — changing it mid-sequence resets
recognition and reads as a different sender.

---

## Emoji: sparingly, never load-bearing

An emoji can earn attention in a crowded inbox but can't carry meaning. Screen
readers announce them aloud ("ticket emoji"), some Windows clients render them
as boxes, and a line that depends on one breaks for those readers.

- At most one, only where it adds tone or a visual anchor.
- At the end, not the start — leading emoji eat the characters that matter most.
- The line must read completely without it.
- **Never on transactional or logistics sends.** "Your bib number is ready 🎉"
  reads as promotional and gets filtered like it.

---

## Measure clicks, not opens

Apple's Mail Privacy Protection (2021) pre-loads tracking pixels for Apple Mail
users, marking a large share of a list as "opened" whether or not anyone read
anything. Open rate is directionally useful at best, and actively misleading
when comparing across segments.

Judge subject lines on **click-through rate and the downstream action** —
registrations, merch orders, photo views. A line that wins on opens and loses on
registrations lost.

Worth stating plainly when it comes up: CTR is a *joint* measure of the subject
line and the email body. A subject line's real job is getting the right person to
open, and the cleanest read on that is click-to-open rate on the non-Apple
portion of the list — imperfect, but less corrupted.

## Testing

- **One variable at a time.** Comparing "Only 200 spots left" against
  "🔥 Prices rise tonight, Kyra!" teaches nothing reusable.
- **Test the category, not the wording.** Word-level tests rarely reach
  significance on an event list. Category-level findings transfer to the next send.
- **Size it honestly.** Detecting a realistic difference in click rate needs
  thousands of recipients per arm. On a list of a few thousand, most "wins" are
  noise. If the test isn't powered, don't run it — choose on judgement and spend
  the effort elsewhere.
- **Decide the metric before sending.** Choosing it afterward is how a programme
  accumulates confident false beliefs.
- **Keep a log.** A running record of category-level results beats any single test.

## Don't do this

| Don't | Why |
| --- | --- |
| ALL CAPS, or "FREE!!!" | Trips spam filters and reads as shouting |
| Multiple exclamation marks | Same, and it undercuts the message |
| "Re:" or "Fwd:" on a cold send | Fake-reply framing is deceptive and permanently erodes trust |
| Fake deadlines | The list learns fast, and urgency stops working for you |
| Clickbait the email doesn't pay off | Buys one click, costs the next twenty |
| Emoji on logistics or confirmation emails | Makes essential information look promotional |
| Unvalidated merge fields | "Hi {{first_name}}" is worse than no personalisation |
| Stacking three techniques in one line | Each weakens the others |
| Burying the point past character 35 | Most of the list never sees it |
