# The lifecycle map

Which category to reach for at each moment in the runner's year. Use this to
pick the three or four categories to generate across.

| Moment | Primary | Secondary | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Early access / pre-sale | FOMO | Personalisation | Lean on the reader's insider status |
| General registration open | Clarity | Social proof | Say what's open and at what price |
| Price-increase deadline | Urgency | — | Show the old and new price |
| Registration closing | Urgency | FOMO | The one moment both are honest at once |
| Sold out | Social proof | Curiosity | Build the waitlist; never imply entries exist |
| Ballot acceptance | Clarity | Personalisation | High emotion, highest open rate of the year |
| Ballot rejection | Curiosity | Pain point | Reframe toward charity entry or deferral |
| Registration confirmation | Clarity | — | Never promotional |
| Training phase (12–4 weeks out) | Pain point | Question | Longest window, most under-used |
| Taper (3–2 weeks out) | Curiosity | Personalisation | Trigger off the runner's own race date |
| Race week | Clarity | Question | Consolidate; assume they're stressed |
| Race morning | Clarity | — | One line, one action |
| Results / photos live | Personalisation | Clarity | Put the finish time in the subject line |
| Merch / photo push | Personalisation | Urgency | Only once the photos have actually landed |
| Early access for next year | FOMO | Personalisation | Reference this year's finish |
| Lapsed (12+ months) | Re-engagement | Social proof | Lead with shared history, not a discount |

## Notes on the hard moments

**Ballot rejection** is the hardest send in the calendar. The instinct is to
apologise. The better move is to change what the email is about — a charity
entry, a deferral, a different distance, next year's early access. Great North
Run's *"Keep your dream alive"* works because it reframes disappointment as
possibility before it asks for anything.

**Sold out** needs care. Don't write urgency or FOMO lines that imply entries
are still available — the reader clicks, finds nothing, and you've spent trust
for a bounce. Pivot to waitlist, to next year, or to the spectator and volunteer
audience.

**Confirmation and race-week emails** are opened at near-universal rates because
the reader needs the information. That makes them the most valuable real estate
in the programme and the worst place to be clever. Say what's inside.

**The training block is the gap worth naming.** Most event programmes
concentrate their best subject lines on registration and race week and send
nothing memorable during the twelve-week build. That block is the longest
sustained window of runner attention in the year, and it's the cheapest place to
build the relationship that makes next year's early-access email work. If a user
asks for help with registration emails and has nothing running during training,
say so.
